Method: scripts/fetch_audio_samples.py + scripts/audio_probe.py. Score = bona-fide logit (higher = more like a real voice); up to 6 windows of 4.04 s per clip,
the lowest window per clip is compared with a cut.
Real (20): Wikimedia Commons speech recordings, mostly OLD or noisy (1895 and 1940s radio, Nehru 1947 speech, Spoken Wikipedia readings, an interview, one real Hindi recording).
  Speech-synthesizer demos and Morse code found by the search were removed by hand.
Synthetic (19): 11 Gemini text-to-speech clips (gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts, English and Hindi, 6 voices) and 8 Windows SAPI clips (David, Zira; older TTS).
Outcome: at every cut that catches most Gemini clips, 18 to 20 of 20 real clips are also flagged. Gemini clips scored HIGHER (more real) than most real clips
(medians -1.7 vs -4.7), SAPI clips scored about -6.2 (flagged, but so were several real recordings).
Reasons it may fail here (not tested): trained on older English synthesis (ASVspoof 2019 LA), our real set is old and noisy and unlike phone voice notes,
the lowest-window rule penalises windows with silence, no Indian-language clones were available.
What this does NOT show: that the model is useless in general (its card reports 11 percent EER on In-the-Wild); only that on our data it gave no usable signal.
Product decision: the check returns scores and no label; the UI says it did not pass our test.
