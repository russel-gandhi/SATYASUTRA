## Goal

Decide a claim label from evidence by plain, testable rules, with no model in the path, so the first verdicts are explainable line by line.

## Done when

- `src/satyasutra/claims/rules.py` takes a `Claim` and its `Evidence` list and returns a `Verdict`. Only Tier 1 or Tier 2 evidence can produce `SUPPORTED` or `REFUTED`. Without such evidence the result is `UNVERIFIABLE_YET`, never a guess.
- A publisher rating counts toward `REFUTED` or `SUPPORTED` only through a rating-to-label table in `config/policy.yaml`. A rating not in the table yields `MISSING_CONTEXT` or `UNVERIFIABLE_YET` and says the rating was unmapped.
- Conflicting Tier 1 or 2 evidence (support and refutation) yields `UNVERIFIABLE_YET` with a `CONFLICT` flag and cites both sides (plan C6 step 4). Support plus a missing-context review yields `MISSING_CONTEXT`.
- A review decides only if its reviewed claim is near-identical to ours (`factcheck_relevance` 0.9, calibrated on 93 live pairs) and the negation matches; the API often returns reviews of the opposite claim. All other reviews are returned as `related` context and never decide.
- Every verdict carries its citations (evidence ids). The schema's coercion already downgrades an unsupported verdict; a test proves the rules never rely on it.
- `tests/unit/test_rules.py` has one test per branch, runs with no network and no model installed.

## Notes

The rating table is data the team fills from real ratings seen in live Fact Check responses (F1-06), not from memory. Start small and let unmapped ratings stay visible rather than adding guesses.
