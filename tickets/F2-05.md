## Goal

Only the busiest clusters reach Gemini and the Fact Check API, one audit per cluster, within the free-tier rate limit. This is the reason the fast clock exists: about 665 posts a minute come in and Gemini allows 15 requests a minute per model.

## Done when

- `src/satyasutra/fastclock/escalate.py` picks the highest-velocity clusters that have not been audited, at most as many per minute as the configured budget allows (derived from `policy.gemini.requests_per_minute` and a reserve left for user requests from `POST /verify`, both in config). A cluster is audited once; the result is stored against the cluster and reused for later members through the verdict cache (F2-03).
- A cluster is eligible only when it passes a velocity floor that comes from `config/thresholds.yaml` (distinct authors per minute and minimum size), chosen from the live capture in F1-08 and re-read here. If nothing passes the floor, nothing is escalated, and the stats say so; the system does not fill the budget with weak clusters.
- The representative text for a cluster is its earliest member, and the audit result carries the cluster id, the number of members and the authors per minute at the time.
- `escalation_share` (escalated posts divided by fast-clock posts) is counted and exposed in `/fastclock/stats`. The Bible's target is at most 5%; the ticket records the measured value, and if it is missed, says so.
- Extraction `blocked` or `failed` results are not retried in a loop: a failed cluster waits before it is eligible again, with the wait from config, and the failure is counted.
- `tests/unit/test_escalation.py` covers: budget respected over a window using a fake clock, one audit per cluster, ordering by velocity, the floor, the backoff, and that user requests keep their reserve when the budget is exhausted.
- `tests/contract/test_live_escalation.py` runs the worker for a short live window and asserts that every escalated cluster has a result or a counted failure, that the number of Gemini calls does not exceed the budget, and prints the measured escalation share.

## Notes

Generic chatter shows almost no spreading clusters, so a quiet window may escalate nothing. That is a valid outcome and the test must accept it; do not lower the floor to produce activity. A staged-news demo is not allowed (no mock data); the demo relies on whatever is really spreading when it is shown.
